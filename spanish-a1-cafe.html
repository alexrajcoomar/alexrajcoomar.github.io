Of the 22 concepts both units teach, 10 carry over from French to Spanish unchanged. The other 12 do not, which is 55 per cent of the shared ground. A second Romance language is not half price.

If you have already done the French unit, the fastest way through this one is to read section 03, drill the 10 misleading pairs until they are automatic, and skim the rest. The 10 that transfer need no work from you. That is the whole argument for reading two units against each other rather than one after the other.

01What this is, and what it is not

A comparison of two A1 café units, not a Spanish course and not a claim about the two languages in general.

The French unit, Unité 1 : Au Café, and this one cover the same ground: the twenty or so words you need to order in a café, the polite request formulas, the article system, two dialogues and a set of drills. Because the domain, the level and the language of instruction are identical, the two units can be laid side by side and the differences are about the languages rather than about how the material happened to be written.

Everything on this page comes from those two units and nothing else. Where a claim would need a third source, it is not made. That rules out a good deal a comparative grammar would say, and it keeps every row on this page checkable against a document you can open.

04Five differences that are structural

Vocabulary you can look up. These five change what a sentence has to contain, so they cost you on every sentence rather than on one word.

French makes you choose an article Spanish does not have

Français

Three sets, and the unit says French makes you choose: indefinite un, une, des for countable items; partitive du, de la, de l' for an unmeasured amount; definite le, la, les for a specific thing. Water and sugar take the partitive.

Español

Two sets: indefinite un, una, unos, unas and definite el, la, los, las. There is no partitive at all, and the unit's article section is about gender and number agreement instead.

What it costs you. A Spanish-first learner under-produces the French partitive and says a bare noun. A French-first learner over-produces de in Spanish, where nothing licenses it.

3.3 A, B and C against 3.3

Politeness sits in a different person of the verb

Français

Formal you is vous, the second person plural. The polite question is vous avez, and the politeness formula itself is s'il vous plaît.

Español

Formal you is usted, which takes the third person singular. The polite question is ¿Tiene?, the same form as he or she has.

What it costs you. A learner who has drilled one conjugation slot for politeness reaches for the wrong slot in the other language, and the error lands on every polite sentence rather than on one word.

Tu vs Vous; 3.1 against 3.2; sociolinguistic notes

Refusing something is one word in Spanish and a grammar rule in French

Français

Je ne prends pas de sucre. The verb is wrapped in ne and pas, and after the negation un, une, des, du, de la and de l' all collapse into de.

Español

Sin azúcar. A preposition, and the noun stays bare.

What it costs you. This is the cheapest sentence in Spanish and one of the first real grammar rules in French. A learner going from Spanish to French produces a bare noun and is understood but visibly wrong.

3.4 against 2; 3.1

The politeness ladders are different heights and disagree about I want

Français

Four formulas: je voudrais, je vais prendre, je prends, je veux. The unit places je veux outside ordering and keeps it for the negative.

Español

Five formulas: the bare noun with por favor, quiero, quisiera, ¿Me da?, and para mí. The unit says quiero is perfectly normal with por favor, and calls ¿Me da? extremely frequent in Mexico and Colombia.

What it costs you. The direct want verb is excluded from ordering in one unit and endorsed in the other. Transfer in either direction produces either needless stiffness or genuine bluntness.

3.1 against 3.1

Both languages make water irregular, in different places

Français

L'eau is feminine and takes the partitive de l' when you order it, because the unit treats water as an unmeasured amount rather than a countable unit.

Español

Agua is feminine but takes el and un in the singular purely for pronunciation, and the plural las aguas shows the true gender. Any adjective stays feminine: el agua fría.

What it costs you. Neither irregularity predicts the other. The only safe move is to learn each as a whole phrase, which is what both units already tell the learner to do with articles.

2; 3.3B against 2, note on el agua

05The article decision, drawn

French asks three questions before it lets you say a noun. Spanish asks two.

This is the single largest divergence in the pair, and it runs in both directions. Coming from Spanish, you will say je voudrais sucre and leave out an article French requires. Coming from French, you will reach for a Spanish partitive that does not exist and produce something like de azúcar where the language wants the bare noun after con or sin.

08What would make this wrong

The honest limits, stated rather than buried.

The classification is a reading. Whether a pair transfers or misleads is my judgement about what a learner will do, not a measured fact about the languages. Someone who already speaks a third Romance language would classify several rows differently. The file is the place to disagree.

The sample is two units, not two languages. Twenty-two shared concepts in one domain at one level cannot support a claim about French and Spanish generally. The proportion that misleads would move with the domain, and almost certainly with the level.

There is no learner in the data. The piece predicts where transfer will fail. It does not observe anyone failing. A real test would be error rates from someone learning the second language after the first, and I have not run one.

Both units were written by the same hand, and so was this. If the French unit is wrong about French, this page inherits the error and presents it as a contrast. The two units are the only sources, which keeps the page checkable and also keeps its ceiling low.
