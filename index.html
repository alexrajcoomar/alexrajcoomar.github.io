Flagged in Hindsight

Five Canadian accounting frauds with completed regulatory or judicial findings — Sino-Forest, Nortel, Poseidon Concepts, Livent, Philip Services — put back through the Beneish M-Score on the filings that existed before each collapse. Zero of three computable test years flag at the standard cut-off, and the piece types every miss by its cause: threshold, mechanism, model scope, or archive failure. Every figure carries a provenance label, and the computation ships with the page.

Crucible Cockpit

A system that refuses to answer questions directly: it decomposes each one into individually falsifiable claims, files a falsifier and an evidence tier with every claim, and sets attack agents against them on provenance, inference, selection, and anachronism. The answer is whatever the surviving claims can carry, at the tier of the weakest load-bearing link. Three runs are on the record — a hindsight-locked backtest and two live audits — with graveyards, rejected attacks, and unabridged transcripts.

The Brittle Network

A research protocol — methodology only, no results — for testing whether the Eastern Mediterranean collapsed around 1177 BCE because exogenous shocks struck a trade network optimised past the point of resilience, with the Sea Peoples as symptom rather than cause. Hypotheses, falsification conditions, data architecture, and the analysis plan are declared before any data is touched, and the abstract to be published if the central hypothesis fails is drafted in advance.

IFRS 15 Judgment Trainer

A drill on revenue recognition under IFRS 15: 29 determinate items scored against the standard and 33 judgment items that show their reasoning, every answer carrying its paragraph or illustrative example, verified against Part I of the CPA Canada Handbook, 2026 Edition. Each item names the trap beside the treatment, because the wrong answer you almost gave is the thing worth studying.

Predictive History

Fifteen systems and 148 couplings, annotated by an audit instrument that was run against its own falsification test and failed it. Every one of eighty claims carries the verdict three independent raters gave it, blind to the author, and a mark for whether its evidence can be retrieved at all.
