## 1. The one fact that should drive everything

The term you are applying for, January to April 2027, is Canadian personal tax
season. You have already worked one. Almost nobody else in the 2B applicant pool
has, because the 2026 Winter term was most of that cohort's first work term and
very few of those terms were billable from week one.

That is not a soft advantage. Every CPA practice in Ontario staffs January to
April against a fixed, statutory, non-negotiable deadline, and a student who
already knows DT Max, already knows what Auto-fill my return does and does not
give you, and has already reviewed 200+ returns against source slips, is worth
materially more to them in that specific term than a stronger transcript with no
season behind it. The allocation below is built on that asymmetry.

## 2. Where the hybrid profile is genuinely unfair, and where it is not

**Against AFM peers.** They have your coursework and many have a better
transcript. What almost none of them have is a public artefact a third party can
re-run. `python3 mscore.py` reprints every Beneish score from a clean checkout.
The Dollarama valuation module recomputes every published figure on each build
and the build fails if the page and the model disagree. That converts
"detail-oriented" from an adjective into a thing an interviewer can verify in
ninety seconds. The IFRS 15 trainer does something rarer still: it refuses to
score the 33 items where the standard leaves a range, which is a demonstration
of professional judgment about judgment, and it is the single most interview-
ready object you own for an assurance conversation.

**Against CS and Math peers.** They can write the code. They cannot read a
deferred tax note, cannot map a leasehold improvement to a CCA class, and have
never seen a T1. For any posting that sits inside an accounting firm or a
finance function, the accounting literacy is the scarce half of the pair, and the
posting was written by someone who knows that.

**Where it does not help, stated plainly.** The portfolio does not fix a
transcript screen, because at large firms the transcript is read by a system or a
screener before a human reads anything else. A high-60s to mid-70s average with
two withdrawals and a retake in progress is the binding constraint at Big Four
campus recruiting and at the pension plans and investment banks in Domain 6,
and no amount of published work changes the order in which those files are
opened. I do not have a verified figure for any firm's actual cut-off and will
not invent one, but the mechanism is not in doubt: the screen runs first.

The strategic conclusion follows directly. **The portfolio's leverage is highest
exactly where a human reads the application before a system does:** small and
mid-market firms, regulators, public sector competitions, and postings written by
the person who will supervise the work. Weight the fifty accordingly.

## 4. Five high-conviction hidden gems

**1. 485743, Cherry Chan Professional Corporation, Agentic Workflow Implementer,
Oakville.** A CPA tax practice hiring someone to automate its tax workflow. You
have a completed DT Max season and 14,719 lines of published Python. The
intersection of those two populations is nearly empty, and this posting sits
exactly in it. Highest conviction of anything in the dataset.

**2. 482437, CloudAct CPA Professional Corporation, Entry-Level AI Agent
Developer, Toronto.** Same structure, same argument. A CPA firm that has decided
it needs a developer will interview the candidate who understands what the
software is for.

**3. 484694, Office of the Auditor General of Ontario, Auditor (Co-op).**
*Flagged in Hindsight* is an ex ante forensic screen run on Canadian cases with
completed regulatory findings, published with the script, reporting a negative
result. That is close to a value-for-money audit in miniature. OPS competitions
also score a written application in a structured way, which is the screening
format that most rewards your profile.

**4. 485574, CIRO, Investigations Intern, Toronto.** Forensic screening plus
securities enforcement is the exact tag pair on your Beneish piece, and you read
the actual regulatory decisions and court records rather than summaries of them.
Small intake, and very few applicants can show investigative work.

**5. 483334, RLB LLP, Systems Developer, Guelph.** A CPA firm's internal systems
developer role. The crossover thesis again, with a bonus: RLB also posts a staff
accounting co-op (483336, in your Domain 1 eighteen), so the firm is already
receptive to SAF students. Risk to note honestly: this posting may screen for a
CS background, so treat it as the highest-variance of the five.

## 6. The tension you should hear before you execute this

Eighteen applications to regional CPA tax practices means the January to April
2027 term will resemble the January to April 2026 term, and you said you do not
want to go back to Taxwide. That objection is fair and I am not going to talk
around it. Three things resolve it.

First, the constraint is real: on a mid-70s 2B transcript with two withdrawals,
holding out for Big Four or buy-side has a materially higher chance of producing
no offer at all, and a second term of nothing is worse for the CPA path than a
second season of tax.

Second, the eighteen are chosen so it is not a repeat. Vine and Partners, RLB,
Wilkinson, S+C, Ward & Uptigrove, Glenn Graydon Wright, MAC, RH Partners, Kreston
and Adams & Miles all run assurance alongside tax. A season at any of them gives
you audit file exposure Taxwide could not, and it gives you a second reference
who is not your previous employer.

Third, the leverage compounds. Two billable seasons plus a published research
platform is a genuinely strong 3A application to the firms that will screen you
out today, and by then the transcript will carry a completed Intermediate
Financial Accounting 1 rather than a withdrawal.

## 7. Sequencing for the next ten days

The applications are not equally expensive to produce. Order them so the
highest-conviction ones get written while you are fresh.

1. **Days 1 and 2.** The five gems and the seven Domain 4 roles. Every one of
   these needs a genuinely custom bracketed sentence in paragraph 2, variant D or
   B. Twelve letters, and they carry the most upside per hour spent.
2. **Days 3 and 4.** Domain 3's eight. Variant B throughout. The four Big Four
   applications will each want their own portal work, so budget for that.
3. **Days 5 to 7.** Domain 1's eighteen and MNP's six. Variant A. These share the
   most language, so the marginal cost per application falls fast, but do not let
   the bracketed sentence become boilerplate. It is the only part they read for
   evidence you know who they are.
4. **Day 8.** Domain 5's six and Domain 7's three. Variants C and B.
5. **Day 9.** Domain 6's two, and a full pass over everything: every posting ID
   correct, every firm name correct in both paragraph 2 and paragraph 4, no
   placeholder brackets left in any document.
6. **Day 10.** Buffer. Submit early on the 16th, not at 08:50 on the 17th.
